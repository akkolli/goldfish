# Goldfish interface design

Goldfish uses a vertical shelf, symbol navigation for destinations, and adaptive rows of colorful cards. Notification Center informs the material, density, and motion while Goldfish keeps its vivid content-type headers and recognizable source icons. [Earlier reference study and implementation record](history/README.md).

## Hierarchy and geometry

- Persistent search, New Clip, and Clear History form one toolbar. The trash button opens a compact chooser for 1 day, 1 week, or all time. Pinned clips and Pinboards are retained. Settings remains available from the application menu and with Command-comma.
- The destination row exposes All clips, Pinned, and user-created Pinboards. Type, order, and search filters remain independent; the filter menu includes sorting. Active filtering has an explicit Clear filters action.
- Navigation is entirely symbol-led. The top controls form one horizontally centered group of 34 pt targets with 8 pt gaps and expose All clips, Pinned, user-created Pinboards, New Pinboard, sort order, refinements, and a contextual reset. A fixed rail centered vertically along the left side of the clip feed exposes all nine content types directly, with 10 pt gaps so neighboring hover and selection capsules remain distinct. There is no destination or category dropdown, and the loose filtered-status row is replaced by the contextual reset symbol. The shelf expands inward by the rail width so card width and the two-column grid remain unchanged. Category colors preserve recognition, and hover tooltips plus accessibility values provide names and scoped counts. The remaining refinement menu uses 36 pt rows for date and source filters and retains keyboard focus until a choice, Escape, or outside click closes it.
- Use 16 pt outer insets, 12 pt card gaps, and 20 pt continuous card corners. Controls have native 28–32 pt targets.
- Compact, Standard, and Wide shelf widths are 336, 420, and 540 pt. Card height is measured from its actual text or media type before selection. A one-line text tile is 116 pt tall; its spanning equivalent is 120 pt. Larger text scales typography by 1.2; section heights round up to preserve complete text lines when AppKit rounds child frames.
- Text tiles show the native character symbol and a numeric count beside the source, pin, and shortcut on the same footer row. Metadata widths are measured so the source uses the remaining horizontal space. Counts use the entire saved text, including spaces and line breaks, rather than the shortened preview or a custom title. Rich text counts its plain-text content, not the attachment path. Unicode characters are respected. Counts are available to VoiceOver and in the card tooltip.
- Standard and Wide shelves pair consecutive compact clips in two aligned columns. Each tile needs at least 180 pt, scaled with larger text; Compact stays one column. Long text, code, and unpaired clips span the row. Cards share a row only when their natural heights are within 36 pt, preventing a short text card from inheriting the empty height of an image. Order stays left-to-right, then downward. Paired cards share a row height, with a 12 pt gutter; fonts retain their reading size. Narrow links and files put their title beneath the identity icon so the text can use the whole tile width.
- Tiles use 18 pt corners, a 44 pt header, a 28 pt footer, and smaller source icons. Spanning cards use 20 pt corners, a 46 pt header, and a 30 pt footer. Media, link, file, and color bodies have separate tile and spanning heights so full-width cards remain dense while previews stay recognizable.
- Left/right placement uses a 12 pt margin inside the usable screen, respecting display offsets and the Dock. Legacy scrollbar gutters reserve space so selection cannot change the card width.
- Card headers retain their full content-type color, type label and age, with the source app icon at the trailing edge. The body shows readable text, code, images, PDF previews, color swatches, and link/file identity. Footers carry the source, optional Pinboard or filename, and quick-paste shortcut. Storage UUIDs and managed attachment paths are not user-facing identities.
- The shelf uses one shared blur and a light neutral tint, with no outer border or window shadow in normal contrast. Cards add a stronger translucent reading surface and subtle boundary. Menus and editors use the same sidebar material and tint. Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast retain opaque surfaces and clear boundaries.
- Semantic neutral surfaces adapt to light/dark appearance. Warm orange identifies Goldfish; system accent identifies selection. Metadata uses stronger adaptive secondary ink.

## Interaction and motion

- Hover uses a short spring to lift and magnify one card while nearby cards gently recede. Layout and hit targets stay fixed. For a single clip, the active outline and Copy/Return target follow the pointer; deliberate multi-selection stays intact while the pointer moves. Up/Down transfers the active outline to the keyboard target. A completed click, accessibility Press, or Return copies the clip. Modified clicks select several clips; dragging does not copy to the system clipboard.
- Up/Down follows the visual rows and retains the preferred column across full-width clips. Left/Right follows chronological order, and Shift extends the selection. Hover proximity follows neighboring rows and columns. Initial rendering fills tall two-column viewports while history remains virtualized.
- Native wheel/trackpad scrolling supports a long virtualized history. Reuse unchanged cards and thumbnails. A new copy must not blink the shelf or pull a scrolled viewport away from the content being read.
- The scrollbar has no contrasting track. Its 4 pt rounded thumb widens on interaction and is centered between the card edge and the shelf's right wall, clear of the cards even during hover. Its 12 pt hit area remains inside that gutter. The card column keeps equal visual margins in overlay and legacy scroller styles; native dragging, accessibility, overlay fading, and the system Always Show Scroll Bars preference remain intact.
- Search scope changes reveal the matching result. Down/Up from search enters the selected result without skipping it; Space can then preview. The focus ring follows the whole search capsule. Destination controls and filters remain available during empty results.
- Opening combines a 280 ms shelf transition with a brief card reveal stagger capped at 48 ms; closing takes 200 ms. Card entrance motion is separate from hover transforms and respects left/right placement. Scroll edges use an alpha mask on the content, revealing the live material instead of painting an opaque strip. Reduce Motion removes the transitions; Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast disable edge fading.
- All actions remain discoverable through labeled native context menus and keyboard equivalents. Pinboard context menus support creation/edit/export/delete; card menus support assignment.
- The shelf has no persistent footer. Copies hidden by the current scope get an explicit Show new recovery action. Undo and capture controls remain available through keyboard shortcuts and application menus.
- Empty states explain the current scope and offer one relevant action. Error, paused, no-results, and no-history states must not imply capture is running when it is not.

## Supporting windows

- Settings uses a stable 820 × 650 pt window, 180 pt sidebar, 28 pt content margins, 25 pt section headings, and neutral groups with 18 pt padding. It resizes down to 740 × 460 pt; long pages scroll. Section switching does not resize the window.
- Shortcut recording, ignored-app editing, capture rules, permissions, storage operations, and destructive confirmations retain native controls and explicit outcomes. Shortcut help is available inside Settings.
- The welcome screen uses the same card language and Goldfish identity. Only Start completes setup and enables future capture; closing it remains a deferral.
- New Clip and text editing use a compact popup beside the compose button. New Clip and the trash popup are mutually exclusive. Hiding the shelf dismisses menus and suspends an editor; reopening restores its draft. Explicit Cancel or toggling off New Clip discards the draft. Pinboard creation uses an attached sheet.
- Opening the nonactivating shelf preserves the destination application. Previews are owned child panels; closing them restores shelf selection, outside clicks close both, and Settings dismisses the shelf. Attached editor sheets preserve unsaved drafts when focus changes.
- Native Quick Look previews do not autoplay media. Web previews retain ephemeral WebKit content, clear navigation, readable address/title hierarchy, loading/error status, and an external-browser action.

## Verification

The optional horizontal layout remains available in Settings, but is not the default or the primary design target. The critical-path suite checks shelf cards, attached composers and menus, privacy, focus ownership, and closing in both layouts. Detailed geometry, appearance, scrollers, accessibility, Settings, onboarding, physical multi-display/Spaces behavior, older macOS releases, and animation quality require the live release checks in [SMOKE_TEST.md](SMOKE_TEST.md).
